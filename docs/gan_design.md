## 1. Executive Summary & Design Rationale
Model 3 explores the adversarial synthesis paradigm on small, highly imbalanced safety crops ($64\times 64$). While plain Deep Convolutional GANs (DCGAN) with binary cross-entropy (BCE) suffer from catastrophic mode collapse and severe discriminator overfitting when trained on limited data, modern GAN advances enable stable, high-fidelity synthesis.

This document details the architectural choices, objective formulations, data augmentation mechanisms, and the rigorous downstream classifier augmentation benchmarking protocol.

---

## 2. Failure Modes & Architectural Solutions

### 2.1 Instability of Plain DCGAN on Small, Imbalanced Datasets
* **The Failure Mode**: Standard DCGAN relies on Jensen-Shannon divergence minimization via Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss. In small datasets ($N_{\text{head}} = 867$), the Discriminator ($D$) memorizes training crops within a few thousand iterations. Gradients passed to the Generator ($G$) vanish ($D(x) \to 1, D(G(z)) \to 0$) or explode, causing irreversible mode collapse.
* **The Modernized Solution (DCGAN Backbone + SOTA Stabilizations)**:
  1. **Hinge Loss**:
     $$\mathcal{L}_D = \mathbb{E}_{(x, y) \sim p_{\text{data}}}[\max(0, 1 - D(x, y))] + \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p_z, y \sim p_y}[\max(0, 1 + D(G(z, y), y))]$$
     $$\mathcal{L}_G = -\mathbb{E}_{z \sim p_z, y \sim p_y}[D(G(z, y), y)]$$
     The hinge loss stops updating $D$ once true images score $> 1$ and fakes score $< -1$, preventing $D$ from over-saturating.
  2. **Spectral Normalization (SN)**: Applied to every convolutional layer in $D$. By constraining the Lipschitz constant $\|D\|_{\text{Lip}} \le 1$, SN guarantees smooth, bounded gradients for $G$.
  3. **Projection Discriminator** (Miyato & Koyama, 2018):
     $$D(x, y) = \psi(\phi(x)) + \langle \phi(x), w_y \rangle$$
     where $\phi(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$ is the pooled feature representation, $\psi$ is a linear scalar scoring unconditional realism, and $w_y$ is a learned class embedding vector. The inner product provides exact class-conditional log-likelihood ratio gradients without artificial label concatenation.
  4. **Class-Conditional Batch Normalization (CBN)** in $G$:
     Feature activations are normalized per layer and scaled/shifted by class-specific affine parameters:
     $$y_{\text{norm}} = \gamma_c \cdot \left(\frac{x - \mu}{\sqrt{\sigma^2 + \epsilon}}\right) + \beta_c$$
     where $\gamma_c, \beta_c \in \mathbb{R}^C$ are generated by a dense embedding from label $y$.
  5. **Two-Time-Scale Update Rule (TTUR)**:
     $lr_D = 4 \times 10^{-4}$, $lr_G = 1 \times 10^{-4}$ with Adam optimizer $(\beta_1 = 0.0, \beta_2 = 0.9)$ to ensure $D$ maintains an accurate gradient field while $G$ converges smoothly.
  6. **Exponential Moving Average (EMA)**:
     Maintain shadow weights $G_{\text{EMA}} = 0.999 \cdot G_{\text{EMA}} + 0.001 \cdot G$. Evaluation, sample grids, and downstream data synthesis strictly use $G_{\text{EMA}}$.

### 2.2 Differentiable Augmentation (DiffAugment)
* **The Failure Mode**: Standard data augmentation applied before $D$ creates a distribution mismatch: $G$ is forced to synthesize already augmented images (e.g. black cutout patches or cut-off heads). If augmentation is applied only to real images, $D$ easily distinguishes fakes by detecting missing augmentation artifacts.
* **The Differentiable Solution** (Zhao et al., 2020):
  A unified differentiable transformation $T(\cdot)$ is applied simultaneously:
  1. To real images during the $D$ update: $D(T(x_{\text{real}}), y)$.
  2. To generated fake images during the $D$ update: $D(T(x_{\text{fake}}), y)$.
  3. To generated fake images during the $G$ update: $\mathcal{L}_G = -D(T(G(z, y)), y)$.
  Backpropagation flows directly through $T(\cdot)$ into $G$.
  
  **Transformations Applied**:
  - `Translation`: Random vertical and horizontal translation of up to $\pm 16\%$ with zero padding.
  - `Color`: Random brightness ($\pm 0.5$), contrast ($[0.5, 1.5]$), and saturation ($[0.0, 2.0]$).
  - `Cutout`: Random square mask of size $20 \times 20$ filled with zeros.

### 2.3 Class Imbalance & Discriminator Overfitting on Rare Class
* **The Problem**: In raw data, `head` constitutes only $3.2\%$ ($N=867$) of training crops compared to $16,041$ helmets and $9,991$ vests. In standard epoch-based shuffling, $D$ sees `head` so rarely that $G$ collapses to trivial modes, or $D$ memorizes the 867 heads and penalizes all non-identical generated heads.
* **The Solution**:
  1. **Class-Balanced Sampler**: Data batches fed to $D$ are sampled with inverse class probability, ensuring equal 1:1:1 class frequency.
  2. **Discriminator Score Tracking**: We continuously log $D(\text{real})$ vs $D(\text{fake})$ separated by class:
     $$\Delta D_c = \mathbb{E}[D(x_{\text{real}}, c)] - \mathbb{E}[D(x_{\text{fake}}, c)]$$
     If $\Delta D_{\text{head}} \gg \Delta D_{\text{helmet}}$, $D$ is overfitting on the minority class.

---

### 2.4 Mode Collapse and Privacy / Memorization Safeguards
* **Face Privacy Risk**: Head crops depict human faces. Synthetic generative models must not memorize or copy-paste real workers' facial features.
* **The Verification & Filtering Protocol**:
  1. **Intra-Class Pairwise LPIPS Diversity**:
     Measure mean pairwise perceptual distance across 200 random samples per class. Low diversity indicates mode collapse.
  2. **Nearest-Real-Neighbor Feature Distance Check**:
     For every generated crop $x_{\text{fake}}$, compute minimum distance to all training crops in feature space:
     $$d_{\text{min}}(x_{\text{fake}}) = \min_{x_{\text{real}} \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}} \| \phi(x_{\text{fake}}) - \phi(x_{\text{real}}) \|_2$$
  3. **Near-Duplicate Exclusion**:
     Any synthetic head crop with $d_{\text{min}} < \tau_{\text{threshold}}$ is flagged as a potential memorization artifact and **strictly excluded from downstream augmentation datasets**.

---

### 2.5 Validation-Driven Model Selection
* Checkpoint selection is conducted strictly on the **Validation Split** using:
  1. **Validation FID** (lower is better, measured against real validation crops).
  2. **Judge Conditional Accuracy** ($\ge 90\%$, verifying class semantics).
  The holdout test split is never touched during hyperparameter sweeps or training iteration selection.

## 3. Downstream Data Augmentation Experiment (Step 3)

The primary research question of Model 3 is:
> *Does synthetic data generation via class-conditional GAN improve supervised classification of rare safety violations over mathematical loss rebalancing and heuristic oversampling?*

### Experimental Protocol
We train the identical supervised classifier architecture (`SmallResNet` from `common.judge`) with identical learning rate ($2\times 10^{-3}$), optimizer (AdamW), epoch budget, and horizontal flip augmentation under **5 controlled conditions across 5 random seeds (42, 43, 44, 45, 46)**:

| Condition | Training Dataset Composition | Class Loss Weighting |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A: Real Only** | Real Train Crops ($867$ head, $16,041$ helmet, $9,991$ vest) | Unweighted ($1.0, 1.0, 1.0$) |
| **B: Real + Class-Weighted** | Real Train Crops ($867$ head, $16,041$ helmet, $9,991$ vest) | Inverse Square Root ($\sqrt{N/CN_c}$) |
| **C: Real + Random Oversampling** | Real Crops + Exact Duplicates of Head (up to $16,041$) | Unweighted |
| **D: Real + GAN-Generated Head (1.0x)** | Real Crops + $15,174$ Synthetic Non-Memorized Head Crops | Unweighted |
| **E1: Real + GAN Head (0.5x)** | Real Crops + $7,587$ Synthetic Head Crops | Unweighted |
| **E2: Real + GAN Head (2.0x)** | Real Crops + $30,348$ Synthetic Head Crops | Unweighted |

### Evaluation & Statistical Significance
- Evaluated strictly on the **Real Holdout Test Split** ($N=7,470$ crops).
- Metrics reported: Raw Accuracy, Balanced Accuracy, Macro-F1, Head Precision, Head Recall, and Head F1 as $\text{Mean} \pm \text{Std}$.
- **Paired Student's $t$-test**: Condition D is compared against mandatory baselines B and C. A generative gain is claimed **only if $p < 0.05$ against both baselines**.
